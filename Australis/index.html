Where to go

Six landscapes that define a country the size of Europe

Australia is less one place and more a dozen. Here are the stops most travellers circle first - each with its own rhythm, light and reason to stay.

New South Wales

Sydney & the Harbour

A masterpiece of 20th-century architecture on Bennelong Point, home to 1,800 shows a year and watched over by millions of visitors - plus the Bridge for sunset walks.

Plan this stop
Queensland

The Great Barrier Reef

Over 2,900 reefs and 900 islands stretching more than 2,300 km - the largest living structure on Earth, visible from space and a World Heritage site since 1981.

Plan this stop
Northern Territory

Uluru & the Red Centre

A sacred sandstone monolith 335 km from Alice Springs, ancient to the Aṉangu people. Watch it shift colour at dawn and dusk - one of the country's most recognisable landmarks.

Plan this stop
North Queensland

Daintree Rainforest

One of the oldest tropical rainforests on the planet, where jungle meets reef. Walk canopy boardwalks and keep an eye out for cassowaries in the morning mist.

Plan this stop
New South Wales

Bondi & the East Coast

Iconic surf, coastal cliff walks and golden sand. Ride the train up from Sydney in minutes - or drive the whole coastline south to the wine valleys of Victoria.

Plan this stop
New South Wales

The Blue Mountains

Sandstone ridges, three-sided canyons and eucalyptus haze that gives the ranges their blue tint. Just an hour west of Sydney - a perfect day-trip or overnight.

How to travel it

Four ways in - pick your pace

There is no single way through Australia. Some people fly, some drive for weeks. These are the four experiences that come back again and again.

Cairns · Whitsundays
01

Dive & snorkel the Reef

The reef supports an extraordinary diversity of life, sheltered by the Great Barrier Reef Marine Park. Take a glass-bottom boat if you can't swim - or drop into 20 metres with a guide.

  • Turtle and reef-fish snorkelling
  • Sail between the Whitsunday Islands
  • Best months: June to October, dry and calm
02

Drive the outback

The Red Centre is all distance and drama. Hire a 4WD, fill your tank at Alice Springs, and chase the sun around Uluru - where rock art, waterholes and ancient stories line every trail.

  • Sunrise & sunset from the base walk
  • Cross to the Olgas at Kata Tjuta
  • Plan fuel stops - services are far apart
Alice Springs · Uluru
Sydney · Port Jackson
03

Sail the harbour city

Few cities reward water as generously as Sydney. Hop a ferry from Circular Quay, watch the Opera House and Bridge slide past, and let the ferries double as your transport between bays.

  • Ferries to Manly & Watsons Bay
  • Catch a show or take an interior tour
  • Sunset cruises run year-round
04

Walk the rainforest

The Daintree pairs tropical jungle with a living reef on its doorstep. Morning is best - cooler, quieter and when the wildlife is out. Bring layers; the mist rolls in fast.

  • Skywalks above the canopy
  • Reef and rainforest in one day-trip
  • Cassowaries, tree-kangaroos & birdlife

When to go

Australia spans three time zones and every season at once. June–October is dry in the north; Dec–Feb brings summer and northern storms.

Money

The currency is the Australian dollar (AUD). Cards are accepted almost everywhere; carry a little cash for outback towns and markets.

Getting in

Most visitors need a visitor visa or eVisitor/eTA. Apply online before you fly - processing is usually quick but never last-minute.

Around the country

Fly between states, rent a car for the regions. The east coast is dense and easy; the outback rewards slow travel with fuel stops planned.
