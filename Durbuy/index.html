Welcome to Durbuy

The Smallest City in the World

Nestled deep within the lush Ardennes forest of Wallonia, Belgium, Durbuy proudly holds the title of the world's smallest city - a distinction it earned in 2009 when its residents voted to upgrade from "village" to "city." With fewer than 700 permanent inhabitants, this enchanting town packs centuries of history into every cobblestone lane.

The origins of Durbuy trace back to the 11th century and Siegfried, Count of Ardennes, who acquired the local castle in 1025. Over the centuries, Durbuy survived wars, fires, and floods - each time rising from the ashes with renewed character. Today, its medieval core remains remarkably intact, drawing visitors from around the globe to wander through streets that have echoed with footsteps for nearly a millennium.

Landmark

Château de Durbuy

Perched on a hill overlooking the town, the castle has been a silent witness to nearly a thousand years of history.

A Thousand Years of History

The Château de Durbuy was first mentioned in historical records dating to the early 11th century. Siegfried, Count of Ardennes - later Grand Duke of Luxembourg - acquired the fortress around 1025, establishing it as a strategic stronghold in the Ardennes region.

Throughout its long history, the castle has been rebuilt and modified multiple times following fires, wars, and changing architectural tastes. Today, its pointed turrets and stone walls dominate the skyline, offering panoramic views of Durbuy's charming rooftops and the surrounding forested hills.

The castle grounds are open to visitors year-round, with guided tours available during peak season. In summer, the plaza below hosts cultural events, markets, and concerts that bring the medieval heart of Durbuy alive.

Nature

The Ourthe River

The Ourthe River winds gracefully through Durbuy, carving its way through the Ardennes landscape and providing a serene backdrop to the town. This gentle waterway has shaped life in Durbuy for centuries - powering mills, facilitating trade, and offering a natural boundary that once protected the settlement.

Today, the riverbanks are beloved by locals and visitors alike. In summer, you'll find people relaxing along the green banks, paddling on calm stretches of water, or fishing beneath ancient stone bridges. The river also serves as a starting point for scenic walks that lead into the surrounding forest.
